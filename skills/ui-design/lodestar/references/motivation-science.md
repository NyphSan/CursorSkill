# Motivation Science — SDT and Octalysis

## Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan) — the scientific backbone

Ryan & Deci (2000), *American Psychologist* — decades of empirical motivation research. **This is the preferred frame**: say "support autonomy/competence/relatedness," not "hack dopamine." Evidence: Strong.

Three innate psychological needs; environments supporting them build intrinsic motivation and wellbeing, environments thwarting them destroy both:

| Need | Definition | Design FOR it | Patterns that THWART it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Autonomy** | Volition; self-endorsement of one's actions | Meaningful choices, customization, multiple pathways, opt-in over opt-out, transparent explained defaults | Coercion, forced continuity, false-hierarchy CTAs, confirmshaming |
| **Competence** | Feeling effective and able to grow | Progress indicators, scaffolded difficulty, micro-achievements, undo/redo, clear feedback | Impossible challenges, unclear errors, streak punishment without repair |
| **Relatedness** | Feeling connected and cared for | Real-connection social features, supportive community, human support paths | Fake social proof, friend spam, forced contact access |

Key finding: controlled extrinsic motivators (pressure, deadlines, contingent rewards) can **crowd out** intrinsic motivation when they thwart autonomy. A retention mechanic that works this quarter can hollow out the motivation that would have retained the user for years.

Applied reading: workspace-construction supports autonomy; progress + appropriate challenge supports competence; clubs/communities support relatedness — and each flips dark when autonomy is removed (hard locks, shame copy, fake social).

## Octalysis (Yu-kai Chou) — gamification taxonomy

Useful design vocabulary, **not** a validated psychometric model (evidence: framework/folklore). 8 core drives:

| # | Drive | White/Black hat | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Epic Meaning & Calling | White | Contribution to something larger; origin narratives |
| 2 | Development & Accomplishment | White | Levels, badges earned through real challenge |
| 3 | Empowerment of Creativity & Feedback | White | Builders, sandboxes, visible results of creativity |
| 4 | Ownership & Possession | Mixed | Avatars, collections, profiles, customization |
| 5 | Social Influence & Relatedness | Mixed | Mentorship, feeds, leaderboards |
| 6 | Scarcity & Impatience | Black-leaning | Exclusive access, appointment dynamics |
| 7 | Unpredictability & Curiosity | Black-leaning | Variable feeds, loot mechanics, sweepstakes |
| 8 | Loss & Avoidance | Black-leaning | Streak loss, expiring rewards |

**Rule: default to drives 1–5. Drives 6–8 require an explicit ethics note and an SDT check every time.** White-hat drives build long-term engagement; black-hat drives create urgency at the cost of burnout and manipulation risk.

## Mapping table

| SDT need | Octalysis drives | Lifecycle stages served |
|---|---|---|
| Autonomy | 3 Creativity, parts of 4 Ownership | Onboarding paths, personalization, refinement |
| Competence | 2 Accomplishment + progress systems | Activation, habit, engagement |
| Relatedness | 5 Social Influence | Growth, trust |
| (Extrinsic pressure) | 6 Scarcity, 7 Unpredictability, 8 Loss | Monetization, retention — **high scrutiny** |

## Companion behavior models

- **Fogg Behavior Model (B=MAP)**: behavior fires when Motivation, Ability, and a Prompt converge — diagnose failures by asking which of the three was missing. Evidence: Moderate.
- **Hook Model (Eyal)**: Trigger → Action → Variable Reward → Investment. Useful loop vocabulary; inherits the variable-reward ethics gate. Evidence: Moderate/framework.
- Both are compatible with SDT when the loop's payoff serves the user's own goal; incompatible when it serves only session count.
